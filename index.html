Profile

Research, information systems and public policy.

I am a PhD candidate at University North, Croatia, in the field of Information Sciences.

My research examines how EU-funded scientific publications receive measurable attention in public policy documents. I distinguish policy attention—the documented visibility of research—from policy impact, which involves the actual use of knowledge in decision-making and cannot be inferred from citation counts alone.

Alongside my doctoral research, I work as a librarian at the Institute for Development and International Relations (IRMO) in Zagreb. My academic and professional interests connect research evaluation, scholarly communication, open science, research information systems and the responsible interpretation of research indicators.

03

Doctoral research

A Harmonization Model for Policy Attention Indicators Toward EU-Funded Research

The dissertation develops a theoretically grounded and empirically validated model for interpreting citations of EU-funded research in public policy documents.

Conceptual distinction

Policy attention is defined as measurable visibility in policy documents. Policy impact refers to actual changes in knowledge or decision-making. This distinction is grounded in Buckland’s tripartite definition of information.

Scale and sources

The study integrates approximately 500,000 Horizon-funded publications and around 20,000 policy-document citations using Overton, OpenAIRE, OpenAlex and CORDIS.

Systematic biases

It examines disciplinary, linguistic, geographical, temporal and academic-impact biases that make raw policy-citation counts difficult to compare responsibly.

Composite indicator

The research develops a composite Policy Attention Index designed to normalize significant bias factors and support more comparable analyses. Its statistical foundation relies on multiple regression modelling and the identification of significant disciplinary, linguistic, geographical, temporal and academic-impact effects.

EU–Croatia comparison

The model is developed on the full EU Horizon corpus and then applied to the Croatian subset to identify country-specific patterns.

Validation

Quantitative findings are triangulated with content analysis and semi-structured interviews with researchers and policy practitioners.
